Step-by-Step Guide to Claiming Legit Crypto Airdrops

Krisna Dwi Saputra 8 min read September 16, 2026

An airdrop is a distribution of tokens to wallet addresses, usually as a reward for doing something useful for a project before it launched. Test a network, provide liquidity, bridge assets, or complete community tasks — these are common actions projects use to spread awareness and reward early supporters.

The appeal is obvious: you can end up with real tokens for nothing but time. The catch is that the same mechanic attracts an enormous number of scammers, and one careless click can expose a wallet or drain it completely.

Step 1 — Set up a dedicated airdrop wallet

Never hunt airdrops with the wallet that holds your savings. Create a completely separate wallet and treat it as disposable.

This single habit is the most valuable thing in this article. If a drainer catches you, it drains the burner — not your portfolio. Fund it only with the small amount needed for gas and basic testing.

  • Use a software wallet you are comfortable with, or a separate hardware wallet if you are farming at scale.
  • Write the seed phrase on paper. Never photograph it, never type it into a notes app, never paste it into a chat.
  • Keep the burner separate from your main wallet. Do not import both into the same browser extension.

Step 2 — Verify the source before you touch anything

Almost every airdrop scam begins the same way: a link that looks right. The domain might be claim-projectxyz.com instead of projectxyz.com, or use a near-identical name that tricks you into thinking it is official.

The rule: never reach an airdrop page through a link someone sent you. Not in Telegram, not in Discord DMs, not in a reply to a tweet you made. Go to the project's official website or verified social profile before connecting anything.

Never do this

If anyone — including an account that looks like official support — asks for your seed phrase or private key to "verify" or "unlock" your airdrop, it is a scam, 100% of the time.

Step 3 — Check eligibility properly

Most real airdrops provide a checker tool where you paste your public wallet address. Pasting a public address is safe — it's public information.

What deserves caution is a "checker" that asks you to connect your wallet and sign a message before it shows you anything. A basic eligibility checker should not need your seed phrase, private key, or a suspicious wallet signature.

If a checker asks you to connect, close the tab and find another route to the information.

Step 4 — Understand what you're signing

When you actually claim, you will be asked to sign a transaction. Before you approve it, check two things:

  1. What function is being called. Your wallet usually shows a plain-language summary. "Claim" or "Approve spending cap" are normal. An unlimited token approval to an unknown contract is not.
  2. Where the transaction is going. Confirm the contract address matches the one published on the project's official documentation.
Watch the approval amount

Many claim flows require a token approval. If the wallet asks you to approve unlimited spending of a valuable token, and the project only needs a small amount, consider whether the request is legitimate.

Step 5 — Claim, then clean up

Once the claim succeeds, don't just walk away. Spend five minutes closing the loop:

  • Revoke approvals you no longer need, using a reputable approval-revocation tool.
  • Move any valuable tokens out of the burner wallet to somewhere safer.
  • Disconnect the wallet from any site you were connected to. Connection alone is low-risk, but there's no reason to leave it open.

The 7 mistakes that cost people their tokens

  1. Using a main wallet. The single biggest cause of catastrophic losses.
  2. Clicking links from DMs. Even from accounts that look verified.
  3. Signing blind. Approving a transaction without reading what it does.
  4. Believing "gas fee" requests. A real airdrop never asks you to send crypto to receive crypto.
  5. Ignoring token approvals. Old approvals to sketchy contracts stay live for years.
  6. Rushing a deadline. Urgency is a manipulation tactic. Fake "claim expires in 10 minutes" timers exist for exactly this reason.
  7. Trusting a screenshot. Anyone can screenshot a fake balance.

The honest expectation

Most airdrop farming produces nothing. You'll spend hours on testnets that never launch a token, or qualify for a distribution that turns out to be worth a few dollars. That's the normal experience, not a sign of failure.

Treat it as a low-cost lottery ticket: spend time, never spend more than you'd be comfortable losing on gas, and never let the possibility of a payout push you into a signature you do not understand.
