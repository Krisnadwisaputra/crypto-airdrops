<p>Testnet farming can be a beginner-friendly way into airdrops because testnet tokens generally have no monetary value. However, some projects may impose eligibility requirements or involve real network costs, so verify the rules before participating.</p>

          <h2>What a testnet actually is</h2>

          <p>A testnet is a parallel copy of a blockchain used for development. Same mechanics, same wallet interface, same transactions — but the tokens are worthless by design, and the network can be reset at any time.</p>

          <p>Developers use testnets to find bugs before real money is at stake. Projects need real people to stress-test their software in ways a small internal team can't.</p>

          <h2>Why projects reward testers</h2>

          <p>When a project eventually launches its token, it needs a distribution. Rewarding the people who showed up early and actually used the product is one of the most credible ways to do it — it builds a real user base instead of a mercenary one.</p>

          <p>Testnet participation can be a useful signal in some airdrop programs because it demonstrates genuine product usage over time. However, no single activity guarantees eligibility, and each project sets its own criteria.</p>

          <h2>Getting test tokens from a faucet</h2>

          <p>You need test tokens to pay testnet gas. These come from a <strong>faucet</strong> — a tool that sends you a small amount for free.</p>

          <p>Most faucets require one of:</p>
          <ul>
            <li>A social account (to limit abuse), or</li>
            <li>A small balance on the mainnet (a Sybil-resistance measure), or</li>
            <li>A captcha and a cooldown timer.</li>
          </ul>

          <p>Always reach the faucet through the official testnet documentation, never through a search result or a link in chat.</p>

          <h2>The workflow</h2>

          <div class="step">
            <div class="step-num">1</div>
            <div class="step-body">
              <h4>Add the testnet to your wallet</h4>
              <p>Networks have RPC URLs and chain IDs. Official docs provide these, or the project site has a one-click "Add Network" button. Never copy these from a random source.</p>
            </div>
          </div>

          <div class="step">
            <div class="step-num">2</div>
            <div class="step-body">
              <h4>Get test tokens from the faucet</h4>
              <p>Paste your public address, complete the requirements, wait for the tokens. They usually arrive in under a minute.</p>
            </div>
          </div>

          <div class="step">
            <div class="step-num">3</div>
            <div class="step-body">
              <h4>Use the actual product</h4>
              <p>This is the part people skip. Swapping once isn't participation. Try the features the project cares about: bridging, providing liquidity, minting, voting, deploying a contract, sending transactions across several days.</p>
            </div>
          </div>

          <div class="step">
            <div class="step-num">4</div>
            <div class="step-body">
              <h4>Spread activity over time</h4>
              <p>A wallet that transacts on 12 different days looks far more human than one that does 40 transactions in an afternoon. Consistency beats volume.</p>
            </div>
          </div>

          <div class="step">
            <div class="step-num">5</div>
            <div class="step-body">
              <h4>Keep a record</h4>
              <p>Track which wallets you used, on which networks, for which projects. When a claim eventually opens, you'll want to know which address qualifies.</p>
            </div>
          </div>

          <h2>How much time does this take?</h2>

          <p>Realistically: 15–30 minutes per project per week, if you're being thorough. Running five projects at once is a part-time job.</p>

          <p>Most testnets never launch a token at all. Of those that do, most distributions are modest. The people who do well at this treat it as a long, boring, disciplined habit rather than a get-rich-quick scheme.</p>

          <div class="callout warn">
            <div class="callout-title">Beware of "testnet" pages asking for mainnet funds</div>
            <p>A testnet faucet should generally provide test tokens without requiring you to send funds to an unknown address. If a testnet faucet or dashboard asks you to deposit, bridge, or pay with mainnet assets, verify the requirement through the project.s official documentation before continuing.</p>
          </div>
