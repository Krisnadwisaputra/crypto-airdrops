<p>Airdrop scams are effective because they don't need to hack anything. They just need you to sign one transaction. No exploit, no breached server, no stolen password — you hand over access yourself, and the blockchain faithfully executes it.</p>

          <p>Understanding the mechanism makes the warning signs much easier to see.</p>

          <h2>How a wallet drainer actually works</h2>

          <p>A drainer is a script hidden behind a "Claim" button. When you connect your wallet and click claim, it doesn't send you tokens. It asks you to sign one of two things:</p>

          <ul>
            <li><strong>A malicious approval</strong> — permission for a contract to spend your tokens. Once granted, the attacker moves them out at their leisure.</li>
            <li><strong>A disguised transfer</strong> — a transaction that looks like a claim but is actually sending your assets elsewhere.</li>
          </ul>

          <p>Both require your signature. Which means <strong>your wallet is the last line of defense</strong>, and the only thing standing between you and an empty balance is whether you read what you're approving.</p>

          <h2>The 9 red flags</h2>

          <h3>1. It arrived unsolicited in a DM</h3>
          <p>Treat unsolicited airdrop DMs as high-risk. If a stranger — or an account you don.t recognize — sends you a claim link, verify the opportunity independently through the project.s official channels before interacting with it.</p>

          <h3>2. The domain is almost right</h3>
          <p>Extra hyphens, a different TLD, a slightly misspelled word. Read the URL character by character. Better yet, don't use their link at all — navigate to the project yourself.</p>

          <h3>3. It asks for your seed phrase</h3>
          <p>This is never legitimate. Not for verification, not for "syncing," not for "unlocking," not for support. There is no exception.</p>

          <h3>4. It asks you to pay first</h3>
          <p>"Send 0.1 ETH to cover gas and receive 5,000 tokens." No. Real airdrops cost you nothing beyond the network fee of the claim transaction itself, which goes to the network — not to a person.</p>

          <h3>5. There's a countdown timer</h3>
          <p>Manufactured urgency is the oldest trick in the book. If a page is pressuring you to act in the next few minutes, that pressure exists to stop you from thinking.</p>

          <h3>6. You never interacted with the project</h3>
          <p>If you've never heard of the project and never used it, why would it reward you? Unsolicited "rewards" for a project you've never touched are almost always bait.</p>

          <h3>7. The wallet prompt asks for unlimited approval</h3>
          <p>Read the approval screen. If a claim requires unlimited spending permission over a token you actually value, stop and reconsider.</p>

          <h3>8. The token appeared in your wallet unannounced</h3>
          <p>Random tokens showing up in your wallet are a common lure. Interacting with them — trying to swap or "claim" them — often triggers the trap. Leave them alone.</p>

          <h3>9. Nobody official is talking about it</h3>
          <p>Cross-check. If a major airdrop were live, the project's official channels would say so. If the only source is the page itself, that's your answer.</p>

          <h2>Your pre-flight checklist</h2>

          <p>Before signing anything, run through this:</p>

          <ul>
            <li>Am I on a wallet that holds nothing valuable?</li>
            <li>Did I navigate here myself, from a source I trust?</li>
            <li>Does the contract address match the official documentation?</li>
            <li>Do I understand what this transaction does?</li>
            <li>Is the approval limited, or unlimited?</li>
            <li>Is anyone rushing me?</li>
          </ul>

          <p>If any answer makes you uncomfortable, close the tab. There will be other airdrops. There will not be another portfolio.</p>

          <div class="callout">
            <div class="callout-title">The one-sentence version</div>
            <p>Many serious wallet-draining attacks require an approval or signature, but other risks can also exist. Slow down whenever your wallet asks for authorization and review exactly what you.re approving.</p>
